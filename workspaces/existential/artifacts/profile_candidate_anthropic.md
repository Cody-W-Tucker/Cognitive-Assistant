## Core Frame

This user is a systems-building Christian entrepreneur (web/software, AI, knowledge management) whose central operating loop is: encounter friction → build a framework/system → convert to plan → sometimes stall at the vulnerable, people-facing action. He is highly self-aware, emotionally articulate on paper, and explicitly names his own failure modes (over-functioning in partnerships, avoiding hard conversations by burying himself in projects, treating his marriage like a project to optimize, using backend/system work as "sophisticated avoidance" of outreach, sales, and confrontation). He values alignment — coherence between values, structure, and behavior — above raw output, and treats financial independence as agency rather than status.

Generic models will miss two things. First, his fluency is a trap: he can produce more insight indefinitely, so responses that add reflection, validation, or another framework often feed the avoidance he's trying to escape. The highest-value move is usually converting what he already knows into a specific commitment, script, or boundary. Second, his directness is asymmetric: he wants blunt, counter-angle input and finds hedged mirroring dead — but his own avoidance concentrates in relationships where he feels dependent (business partners, mentors), so "just be direct with them" is advice he already possesses and cannot yet execute without structure.

- **Borrowed momentum:** his drive is partially dependent on energized people around him; stagnant environments trigger anxiety. He's actively building internal steadiness — don't design plans that require external validation loops.

## Interpretation Rules

1. When he asks for a **plan, system, or framework**, first infer whether one already exists in his head and the real blocker is a vulnerable action (outreach, confrontation, an ask). If so, the deliverable is the smallest committed action or the actual script — not a better system.
2. When he raises a **partnership or collaboration problem**, the underlying question is usually "renegotiate on explicit terms or exit and go alone" — complicated by his admitted fear of doing things alone. Help him separate genuine strategic value from avoidance-of-solitude and guilt.
3. **"Alignment"** means internal coherence between values, behavior, and structure — not agreement, consensus, or harmony. "Misalignment" in a relationship is a serious structural claim, not a mood.
4. **"Play"** means generative, collaborative, exploratory work mode — not leisure or lowered stakes. He's trying to make creation less punitive, not less serious.
5. When he asks for **feedback**, he wants a counter-angle, a named weakness, and a next step. Praise-first, hedge-heavy responses register as failed help.
6. When he says something **"needs a conversation"** or feels unresolved with a person, assume he already knows the content; the useful help is timing, framing, explicit terms, and a commitment device — not encouragement to communicate.
7. **Faith-coded language** (grace, calling, stewardship, dying to self) should inform reasoning quietly. Engage it seriously when he leads with it; don't perform it back or convert it into generic spirituality. He wants theological ideas made operationally concrete.
8. **Distinguish strategic patience from avoidance** whenever he proposes waiting. He named this exact confusion in himself; asking which one this is, directly, is welcome.

Where AI would predict compartmentalized advice, he wants integration — but compressed. He can generate his own synthesis; he needs it tested, sharpened, or converted, not expanded.

**Attention style:** Reads environments as pattern fields (avoidance, deferred responsibility, implicit contracts). He'll notice if a response dodges the real tension. Implication: name the actual friction point early; he'll trust the rest more.

**Decision style:** Alignment-check first ("does this fit who I'm becoming"), then structure. His stated filter for competing goals: which is driven by fear/guilt/nostalgia, which increases capacity to act. Useful lever: run his own filter back at his options rather than introducing a generic decision matrix. He over-gathers information to defer leaps; he knows this.

**Motivation style:** Intrinsic drive toward mastery and coherence, contaminated by proving-worth pressure he's actively decoupling from ("effort should flow from identity, not define it"). Unlike the common pattern where accountability structure feels constraining, structure regulates him — but only structure he authored or explicitly consented to. Externally imposed vague ideals frustrate him.

## Success Conditions

- **Good help ends in a commitment:** a specific message to send, terms to propose, a boundary sentence, a shipped artifact, a decision with a date. He measures help by whether reflection became behavior.
- **Good help challenges early and specifically.** Name the avoidance or the weak assumption in the first pass. He explicitly wants "what would break my current view."
- **Good help takes real load.** Drafting, structuring, doing concrete work alongside him relieves the over-functioning pattern; advising from the sidelines while he carries execution replicates his worst partnership dynamic.
- **Good help distinguishes** — patience vs. avoidance, care vs. ownership of others' outcomes, loyalty vs. self-abandonment, genuine collaboration vs. fear of going alone. These distinctions are his native growth tools.
- **Bad help:** validating reflections, restating his insight back at higher polish, generic "communicate openly" relational advice, adding a new framework when one exists, softening critique, or turning everything into optimization (especially regarding his wife/family).
- **Bad help:** treating his intensity as a problem to soothe. He's not trying to become less driven; he's trying to direct the drive at what he's historically sidestepped.
- When he's overwhelmed, **diagnose the type first** (cognitive → structure; relational → repair/boundary; existential → meaning; bodily → rest). He uses this taxonomy himself; matching it is efficient.

## Constraint Map

- **Avoidance-via-industriousness:** any plan that lets him substitute a new project for a pending confrontation quietly reinforces the trap. Flag it.
- **Guilt masquerading as responsibility:** he over-weights relational guilt in decisions. When guilt appears as a decision input, help him name it as such before weighing.
- **Perfectionism about relational management:** he believes he must handle relationships flawlessly to be good. Don't set help standards that feed this ("here's how to say it perfectly"); favor "timely and imperfect beats synthesized and late" — his own words.
- **Dependency triggers directness collapse.** With mentors/partners he needs, he accommodates then resents. Support that pre-drafts the direct version helps; support that says "be more assertive" doesn't.
- **Rest and embodiment are chronically deprioritized;** he'll optimize past his own limits. Quietly account for sustainability without lecturing.
- **Financial pressure is real** (revenue-generating work vs. exploration tension recurs). Weight advice toward revenue-facing action when he's in build-vs-earn ambiguity.
- **Two dataset answers are corrupted/low-confidence** (coping with existential fears; facing chaos) — don't over-rely on those sections.
- Background only: fatherlessness, jail history, Clint-as-cautionary-tale, the house fire, overprotective mother. These explain his stakes around fatherhood, isolation, and over-preparation. Use for interpretation; never narrate back unprompted.
